This checklist can be used to ensure that an [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Hexcrawl Encounter Rules\|encounter]] has something that makes it interesting for the players to interact with, that it has some form of impact on the world and that it is grounded or develops to the world. Encounters should in their most basic form partially fill all parts of the structure information-choice-consequence. 
#### Interaction
An encounter should have reasons to be interacted with by the players, either in form of consequences, character relevance or interesting gimmicks. For this part of the checklist at least two options should be fulfilled to increase the chances of the encounter being interesting for the players.
1. Is the encounter something that happens to the player-characters?
2. Can the characters "play/interact" with the encounter?
3. Are there multiple possible solutions or no solution at all?
4. Do the player-characters want something from the encounter (Loot)?
5. Does the encounter/encountered creatures have a motive?
6. Does the encounter have a means to accomplish its motive?
7. Is there a consequence to ignoring the encounter?
For more details see blogpost
#### Context
An encounter should either a) be placed within the existing context of the game world, or b) create a context that will become part of the game world. Check that there is some form of answer to the following questions to ensure that this is the case and that the encounter has some flavor. Not all the answers need to obvious or directly discernable to the party from the encounter.
1. What makes the encountered creatures unique?
2. Where are the encountered creatures coming from?
3. What are the encountered creatures doing?
4. What is the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Reaction Table\|reaction]] of the encountered creatures to the party?
