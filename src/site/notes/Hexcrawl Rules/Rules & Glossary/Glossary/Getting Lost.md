Characters trying to [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Regional Hexcrawl Rules\|enter new hex]] must make a navigation check, most often using their Wisdom (Survival), against the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Terrain Table\|Terrain DC]] while factoring in bonuses and penalties from any [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Landmark\|Landmark]] or [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Weather Conditions\|Weather Conditions]]. A player can ask to use a different skill to make the navigation check if it is relevant. For example trying to find a magical anomaly could be done using Intelligence (Arcana) or they might use Wisdom (Animal Handling) after casting Speak With Animals and asking the local wildlife for directions. 
### Determining the Navigation DC and Bonuses
Determining the Navigation DC and bonuses to rolls is usually done when the party tries to enter a hex. It is done is in four steps:
 1. Look at the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Terrain Table\|Hexcrawl Terrain Table]] and use the Navigation DC of the terrain type that matches the hex the party is trying to enter.
 2. Look at the two hexes that shares a border with both the target hex and the party's current hex. For each of those hexes that shares a terrain type with the target hex increase the DC by 1. If the party's current hex and target hex are of the same terrain type, increase the DC by 2.
 3. Apply any effects from the currently active [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Weather Conditions\|Weather Conditions]] and calculate the current visibility of the party.
 4. If the party can see a [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Landmark\|Landmark]] apply the following bonuses depending on their relative direction of travel. Landmarks grant a +5 bonus to any relevant navigation check made whilst traveling straight towards them, and they provide a +3 bonus while traveling in any other direction.
### Failed Navigation Check - Veer
On a failed navigation check the party becomes lost and may veer away from their intended target hex. If there is a hex of the same terrain type as the target hex that shares a border with both the target hex the party is trying to enter and the one they are leaving, the party enters that hex instead, the direction of the tile is the direction the party starts to veer. If there both those hexes share the terrain type of the target hex or if there are no hexes of a shared terrain type, roll a d10. On a 1-4 the party veers left, on a 5-6 they veer straight and enter the target hex, on a 7-10 they veer right.

Once a part has begun to veer they will continue traveling in that direction until they realize they are lost. A veer can increase but never decrease in angle, on repeated failed navigation checks roll the d10 again, for example, if the party is veering right and rolls a 1-4 they do not veer left but rather continue traveling in the same direction. A straight veer can veer left or right but never back the other direction once it has started to veer left or right.
#### Optional - Simplified Veer
On a failed navigation check the party enters a hex that borders both the hex they are trying to enter, the target hex, and the one they are leaving. If either hex shares a terrain type with the target hex they enter that hex. If neither does check if either hex shares a terrain type the hex the party is leaving. If neither or both hexes fulfills these conditions roll a d6, on an odd result they enter the left hex, one an even result they enter the right hex. Repeat this process for each failed navigation check.
### Recognizing You're Lost
Once a lost character makes a successful navigation check upon trying to enter a new hex they will realize they are lost after they enter that hex. 

Characters who encounter a clear landmark or unexpectedly enter a distinctly different type of terrain can make an additional navigation check against the new Terrain Navigation DC to realize that they’ve become lost.
#### Reorienting
A party has realized that they are lost have several options to reorient themselves:
1. **Familiarity:** If the party enters a hex that they have fully explored or that contains a known trail or previously visited location, the party knows where they are. If the party enter a hex they have previously entered, without any of the above conditions being fulfilled, the party makes the navigation check with advantage and thinks the area feels familiar. 
2. **Backtracking**: A lost character can follow their own tracks ([[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Watch\|see the Find Tracks watch action]]). While tracking allows them to retrace their steps, they must still recognize the point at which they went off-track. If a character is successfully backtracking, they may make a navigation check (using the Navigation DC of the terrain) each watch. If the check is successful, they’ll correctly recognize whether they were previously on-track or off-track. If the check is a failure, they reach the wrong conclusion.
3. **Compass Directions:** It requires a DC 10 Wisdom (Survival) check to determine true north without a compass or similar device. On a failed check, randomly determine the direction the navigator thinks is true north with a d8.
4. **Setting a New Course:** A lost character can attempt to precisely determine the direction they should be traveling in order to reach a known objective or [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Finding Locations\|Location]] by making a navigation check at the Navigation DC of the terrain + 10. If the navigator fails the check, they immediately become lost. Determine their direction of travel like any other lost character.

**Alternative Rule – Group Check**: Instead of only one character making the checks, the rules for group checks can be used. If at least half the group succeeds on their Wisdom (Survival) checks, they have determined the correct direction of travel. If not, they immediately become lost.
