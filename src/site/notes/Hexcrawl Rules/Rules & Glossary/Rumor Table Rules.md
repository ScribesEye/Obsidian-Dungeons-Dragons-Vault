The [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Rumor Table\|Rumor Table]] exist to provide players with information about locations, creatures and ongoing events to help them set goals, ask specific questions and plan their future expeditions. 
### Using the Rumor Table
The rumor table is a random table using a d10, d12 or d20 to provide tangible information at a controlled pace in order to give the information meaning and not overwhelm players. The frequency at which rumors are given can be controlled by deciding what trigger a roll and what the players can do to discover rumors.
#### Triggering the Rumor Table
Different events or player actions can trigger a roll on the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Rumor Table\|rumor table]], preferably rolled by the players, and will result in the players learning one rumor. Triggers can be sorted into three main types: proactive, reactive and opportunistic.
##### Proactive
Proactive triggers serve as a means to deliver rumors without needing player action, enabling them to push or guide players in new direction, some examples include:
- **Interval Trigger**: At a regular interval, such as a new [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Clock Cycle\|Clock Cycle]] of the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Faction Clock\|Faction Clock]] or the start of every week, a new rumor is learned.
- **Action/Location Trigger**: Based on player actions not aimed at learning rumors, such as each time players return to their [[Hexcrawl Rules/Sanctuary/Sanctuary\|Sanctuary]], visit a town or take a rest with another party/people not from the party. 
- **Character Creation**: Giving brand new characters a rumor to give them a direction and attachment to the world and enable early goal setting.
- **Rumor Check**: At the start of a new session when a character has not been played for a while, roll a d4 to determine how many rumors they have heard since the last time a player played them. 
##### Reactive
If players want to seek out rumors on their own they actively look for them or search for information that could be potential content of rumors. To do this they could take some of the following actions. 
- **Investigate Action**: Players can spend a watch to scour a community for information and make a Charisma (investigation) check to attempt to learn rumors from the locals. A high result can be rewarded with additional information or understanding of the truth value of the discovered rumors.
- **Tavern Talk**: Spending a day or night drinking in the tavern and buying the customers a round of drinks is a good method to hear the local rumors.
- **Broadsheets & Bulletin Boards**: The local equivalent to newspapers, town criers and bulletin boards should have a few rumors, maybe bounties, job offers, notable headlines or cryptic prophecies.
- **Research**: Visiting the local library or sifting through discovered books might not reveal much about current events but can contain rumors based on the antecedents to current events, knowledge of old locations and treasures.
Adding a cost to players actively gathering rumors can be added but should be fairly low unless the players are paying a reliable information broker to gain information. Costs could be for library entrance, buying drinks or bribes.
##### Opportunistic
The purpose of rumors is to deliver information and hooks to the players and if an opportunity to deliver them presents themselves they can be used, some examples of when a rumor roll could be triggered are:
- Broad social interactions such as the players spending a night at the tavern or carousing around town or training with the local guards.
- Specific Character interactions can be a moment to use the rumor table in order to generate a conversation topic. 
- Interrogation or questioning of NPCs about a specific location or creature.
##### Contextualizing Rumors
The delivered rumors don't need to have a story of how the information was learned but it can be a fun moment to invite players into roleplay and world building. Playing through how the players got a rumor can add a face to the caravan that was attacked by goblins or introduce a retired guard looking for work. In general the following steps can be taken: 
1. Summarize how the players are looking for the information.
2. Frame the key moment where the information was retrieved (can be played out).
3. Contextualize the information using cues from the situation, character and timing from when the rumor was found to attach the rumor to a specific perspective from within the world.
If there is any uncertainties, ask the player how their character might how found the information or provide some ideas. Were they reading the bulleting board, a letter, talking to a friend or visiting a fortune teller.
### Components of a Rumor
Each rumor needs to have the following four components, a source, focus, type and truth value. Each rumor should be Actionable, it should give a clear location for the players to go to, and ensure that the players can do something with the rumor. The degree to which a rumor is actionable can vary, stating a location lies west of X is less actionable than stating Y creature is in Z location. 
#### Source
Where does the rumor come from, not who delivers the rumor. Rumors can potentially come from the following:
- Hexes
- Random Encounter Tables
- Roads/Paths/Trails
- Factions
- NPCs
This means that anything that exists in the hexcrawl can be used a source of a rumor. 
#### Focus
To decide what information and which details a rumors contains it needs to have a focus, which can include:
- Location
- Creature
- Object
- Actions/Situations (including threats or upcoming faction clock triggers)
- Background/Lore
For examples see [link](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/48539/roleplaying-games/hexcrawl-tool-rumor-tables). The details should be minimal to make the rumor easy to remember and easy to figure out which information in the rumor is actionable. 
#### Type
A rumors type categorizes it content into five main types in order to make rumors more clear, provide them with a purpose, value and/or risk. A rumor can be one of the following five types:
- **Local Color**: Tells the players about an area or the people/monsters who live there. The information might no be inherently actionable as it often lacks a specific motivation to go to the location of the rumor, as such it can be given its own table or mixed with other rumors. (Example, The original ship of Theseus lies north of Epirus or the Aenean priestess is named Circe who lost her hand to a wyvern 10 years ago).
- **Opportunities**: Inform players about a reward, payoff or clear gain. Can be treasure, gold bounties and/or magic items but can also be land, favors, materials or chances to interact with new characters. 
- **Challenges**: Similar to opportunities but with additional threats or obstacles that players need to overcome to gain the rewards. Sometimes the reward can be less clear, maybe it is an opportunity to be a hero, spread the name of the party or defeat a shared enemy. (Examples, goblin infested mines, haunted for with alchemical ingredients, or a wyvern is attacking the southern trade routes) 
- **Dangers**: Challenges but without a clear reward, rather than something to be approached these rumors are something to avoid, at least until the party becomes higher level. Can also inform about dangers or traps hidden in a location, along a path or amongst treasures to help players avoid mistakes or losses.
- **Mysteries**: Opportunities where the reward is learning secret lore or unraveling the unknown. Meant to appeal to players curiosity and if investigated give information that allows players to better navigate the region, its factions or understand the anomalies within the region.  
#### Truth Value
Even a false rumor can lead to an interesting adventure. A rumor will not always be true, or at least not entirely true, as its contents are not the same as reality. A rumor can be:
- True
- False
- Partial (There is a lumberjack in the Aenean mountains, but the rumor didn't say he was a murderhobo)
- Mixed (There is a friendly lumberjack in the Aenean mountains, true but he is not friendly)
How true and reliable a rumor is may be judged by how it is acquired, some providers of rumors might clarify if they think the rumor is true or not in order to maintain the players trust. Allowing a mix of rumors encourages thought and consideration before acting on a rumor, but following a rumor should always be interesting, regardless of it gives you a reward or leads you to a goblin ambush.  
### Filling the Rumor Table
Once a rumor has been passed on to the players it needs to be replaced by a new rumor. If desired, the pace at which rumors are replaced can be tied to in game time, such as one new rumor being added at the start of each [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Clock Cycle\|Clock Cycle]] of the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Faction Clock\|Faction Clock]] for the players faction. 

When generating rumors dice can be used to help or generate them on the fly. First determine a random hex on the hexmap by rolling two dice, then roll a dice for each component of the rumor to determine its content. 

##### Refreshing Rumors
If a rumor has been on the table for more than 5 faction clock cycles, check if anything in its affected hex has changed. Alternatively if a change occurs to the hex a rumor mentions make the appropriate changes to the rumor, likewise if a creature mentioned in a rumor has been slain.
