[[Hexcrawl Rules/Appendixes/Regional Hexcrawl Appendix\|Appendix]]
The Regional [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl\|Hexcrawl]] aims to provide an engaging system for making meaningful and informed decisions during travel, exploration and/or conquest of a region. The foundation of the Regional Hexcrawl is a [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexmap\|Hexmap]] that the players explore and fill out with information over the course of their adventures. 
### Regional Hexes
A [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hex\|Hex]] on a Regional Hexmap is three miles across one side to another opposing side, this distance is called a *League*. All movement across the hexmap is measured in hexes, not miles or hours and there is no traveling half a hex, only entering and exiting a hex. On a regional map players can see into the six adjacent hexes, gaining information about their terrain type and a hint about any obvious visual features or structures. Time can be measured in [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Watch\|Watches]], with each watch representing 4 hours.
#### Entering a Hex
When the party enters a hex, consult the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Terrain Table\|Terrain Table]] below (the table assumes a Normal Exploration Pace). A hex's terrain type determines how much time it takes to travel into that hex. A character on horseback moves twice as fast when entering a road, plains, forest (sparse), moor or hills hex. Crossing a river adds one hour of travel time if not on a road or a hex with a bridge.

When the party enters a hex they must make a [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Getting Lost\|Navigation Check]] normally a *Survival* (Wisdom) check but other alternatives can be used when appropriate. The DC of the check is affected by nearby hexes, [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Landmark\|Landmarks]] and [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Weather Conditions\|Weather Conditions]]. If the party fails they [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Getting Lost\|Get Lost]] and travel into a reasonable adjacent hex instead. If the party is traveling along a [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Trail\|Known Trail]], river, coastline or treeline then any navigation checks made automatically succeed.

Upon entering a hex the players are given a description of the hex as well as any unexplored adjacent hexes. Each character may make one [[Arrival Check\|Arrival Check]] to attempt to learn details about the current hex or find hidden locations and secrets in the hex. 
##### Returning to a Hex
If a character has entered a hex by making a successful navigation check they have a +5 bonus to navigation check to enter that hex in the future.
#### Exploring a Hex
A party can spend some time to thoroughly explore the hex they’re in. Doing so takes the same amount of time as entering that hex with a normal pace in the current weather conditions would have, though being on horseback confers no benefits here, and reveals any hidden locations and/or trails that might be in a hex. This also provokes a roll on the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Overclock\|Overclock]] unless done using the time it would take to enter the hex at slow pace and succeeding on a DC 15 Wisdom or Dexterity (Stealth) group check.
#### Exploration Pace
There are three main [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Exploration Pace\|Exploration Paces]] a party can choose to adopt while traveling, each having a different effect on events, stealth and navigation. Traveling at a fast pace requires the party be following an already [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Trail\|Known Trail]], either one they have followed before or one they have made themselves. 
##### Fast Pace
When entering a hex with a **known trail**, the party can move quickly through the wilderness, parties traveling at fast pace:
- Cannot use Stealth checks to avoid detection.
- Suffer disadvantage to Wisdom (Perception) and Intelligence (investigation) checks.
- Rolls the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Clock Dice\|Clock Dice]] of any [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Event Clocks\|Event Clocks]] with disadvantage (subtract highest)
##### Normal Pace
An party traveling at normal pace cannot use Stealth checks to avoid detection.
##### Slow Pace
While moving at a slow pace, the party is purposely being careful. An expedition traveling at slow pace:
- Gains advantage on navigation checks.
- Can make Stealth checks to avoid detection.
- Rolls the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Clock Dice\|Clock Dice]] of any [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Event Clocks\|Event Clocks]] with advantage (subtract lowest)
- One character can the the Cover Tracks Watch Action (See below).
##### Trailblaze
[[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Trailblaze\|Trailblazing]] is a special exploration pace which can be taken in conjunction with other travel paces. Trailblazing halves the traveling speed of the party but also marks an efficient [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Trail\|Trail]] through the wilderness with some form of signs – paint, simple carvings, cloth flags, etc. 

Entering a hex while trailblazing provokes an additional roll of the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Overclock\|Overclock]] clock dice. Once blazed, this trail is considered a known trail to the party. A party can also choose to Trailblaze a hex they are in as if exploring that hex, this also provokes a roll on the Overclock.

When blazing a trail, the character making the signs can make a Wisdom (Sleight of Hand) check to disguise them so that they can only be noticed or found with a Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation) check. The result of the Wisdom (Sleight of Hand) check is the DC to find the trail.
##### Optional Rule - Trailless Fast Pace
Parties can also travel at a fast pace without following a trail, when do doing so parties:
- Cannot use Stealth checks to avoid detection.
- Suffer disadvantage to Wisdom (Perception) and Intelligence (investigation) checks.
- Suffer a -5 penalty to navigation checks.
- Rolls double the amount of [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Clock Dice\|Clock Dice]] for any [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Event Clocks\|Event Clocks]] and makes the rolls with disadvantage (subtract highest)
- Cannot Trailblaze.
### Exploration Day
At the start of every day, every character must consume one ration or an equivalent amount of food. After that, the DM rolls for weather to determine any applicable modifiers to the day’s navigation checks. The day is split up into six [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Hexcrawl Watch\|Watches]], each four hours long and there are four types of watch:
- Active
- [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Hexcrawl Resting Rules\|Rest]]
- Travel
- Camp
While traveling, it is generally assumed that a party is spending two watches per day traveling, two watches per day resting, one watch managing camp and one watch per day engaged in other activities. We assume 2 hours of the camp watch are consumed in the morning having breakfast, getting dressed and preparing for the day’s exploring, and the other 2 hours in the evening on making camp, eating dinner and generally winding down after a long day. 
##### Forced March
A full day of exploration is 8 hours long, and any time spent exploring beyond that risks driving one or more members of the party to exhaustion. Whenever a party finishes entering or exploring a hex, if they have spent more than 8 hours exploring that day, tally up the additional hours spent and ask each character to make a Constitution saving throw. The DC is 10 + 1 for each hour past 8. Each character that fails suffers one level of exhaustion.
### Watch Actions
During a usual traveling day there is one watch, the active watch, that a character can use as they wish, provided they didn’t march beyond 8 hours. Here are some examples of downtime activities they could spend their time on:
##### Forage 
Characters can forage during an active watch. Foragers make a Wisdom (Survival) check against the Forage DC of the terrain. On a success, the forager either gains 1 ration of food or finds a source of fresh water (allowing the party to drink their daily ration of water and for waterskins to be refilled). An additional ration of food or source of fresh water can be found for every 2 points by which the check result exceeds the DC. A food ration found by foraging can be used to cook a meal for one character.
##### Cook
Characters can take the time to prepare a full warm meal using rations foraged during the same day. Each ration consumed allows one character to recover 1 additional hit dice during their next long rest.
##### Craft Mundane Item
Characters can Craft Mundane Items during an active watch, reducing the time remaining to craft an item they’re working on by half a day, provided they have sufficient resources.
##### Craft Magic Item
Characters can Craft Magic Items during an active watch, reducing the time remaining to craft an item they’re working on by half a day, provided they have sufficient resources.
##### Cover Tracks
A character can attempt to cover the party’s tracks. This is a stealth action, that can be taken if the party is moving at a slow pace. The action can also be taken during an active watch to attempt to cover any tracks left behind in the current hex (including from the camp). The character makes a Wisdom (Stealth) or Wisdom (Survival) check to set the Track DC for any pursuer attempting to find or follow their tracks. The check suffers disadvantage in very soft ground, but gains advantage on hard ground. Condition modifiers apply normally to the pursuer’s tracking check
##### Find Tracks
A Character can attempt to find tracks by making a Wisdom (Perception) check against a [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Track DC\|Track DC]] to attempt to follow creatures or find trails leading into nearby hexes. 
If successful the tracker can make a Wisdom (Survival) check against the track DC to attempt follow it, making a new roll each time a new hex is entered. 
##### Scout Ahead
A character can scout ahead to attempt to learn more about the lay of the land and help chart a course. If the navigator of the party receives a scout’s report or the scout uses the information themselves, they gain advantage on their navigation checks for the next travel watch (optional, change to +3 bonus).
##### Optional Rule - Sighting
A character can spend an active watch finding a good outlook in the current hex and attempt to learn more about the nearby hexes. A character taking this action makes a Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation) check to attempt to find a good location, on a success they can see the terrain type of the hexes beyond the adjacent hexes, if they fail they only see into the adjacent hexes. The character can attempt to make a Wisdom (Perception) check to discover any notable features in the adjacent hexes but takes a -5 penalty to the check due to their distance.
##### Short Rest
A character can spend their Active Watch taking a [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Hexcrawl Resting Rules\|Short Rest]] (See below)

### Food & Water
Characters and animals need food and water during their travels, most of this is calculated using rations and waterskins as well as results from forage checks.
#### Food
Small or Medium creatures require 1 ration of food per day. They can go without food for a number of days equal to 3 + their Constitution modifier (minimum 1) before suffering 1 exhaustion level per day thereafter. A normal day of eating resets the count of days without food to zero. 

A creature on half rations counts as going a ½ day without food (and these half days accumulate until they can eat full rations).
#### Water
Small or Medium creatures require 1 gallon (4 liters) of water per day, or twice that in hot weather. A creature on a half ration of water must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution check at the end of each day or suffer a level of exhaustion. If they drink less water than that, they suffer a level of exhaustion automatically. If the character already has one or more levels of exhaustion, the character takes two levels instead of one level in either case.

Waterskins hold a half-ration of water.

**Recovery**: Any exhaustion suffered from lack of food or water cannot be removed until after a full day of normal consumption.

**Large & Tiny Creatures**: Tiny creatures require one-quarter ration of food and water per day. Large creatures (like horses) require four rations  of food and water per day.
#### Sparse Biome
At the GM’s discretion, a biome may be deemed sparse, mostly in deserts. In a sparse biome, each source of fresh water discovered only yields one gallon of water.
### Resting
While exploring characters can gain the benefits of one Short and one Long Rest every 24 hours. Due to the challenging conditions that travel and exploration incurs good rest is harder to achieve than at a settlement. Some abilities such as innate abilities from a characters species recover at dawn every day rather at the end of a long rest, reflecting their source being nature. 
#### Short Rest
A short rest takes one Watch (4 hours) to complete and is interrupted by rolling initiative, taking damage or casting a spell with a spell slot. After completing a short rest characters may:
- Spend a hit dice to roll the dice and regain hp equal to the result plus the characters Constitution modifier.
- Regain a use of an ability that recovers on a short rest (option, half or full proficiency bonus uses).
- Spend a hit dice to recover proficiency bonus amount of uses of short rest abilities. (Warlock 1 slot equals one hit dice, paladin 1 use of lay of hands equals 5hp).
Taking a short rest will most likely provoke a roll on the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Overclock\|Overclock]] or [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Underclock\|Underclock]]depending in where it is taken. 
#### Long rest
A long rest takes two Watches (8 hours) 6 of which must be spent sleeping and 2 of which may be light activity. To gain the benefits of a long rest it needs to be taken in conjunction with the Camp Watch Action otherwise it becomes a poor quality long rest (see below). After completing a long rest a character may:
- Regain up to half of their maximum hit points.
- Reduce their level of exhaustion by 1.
- Regain all uses of their abilities that recover on a short rest.
- Spend one hit dice to roll the dice and regain hp equal to the result plus the characters Constitution modifier.
- Spend a hit dice to regain a number of uses equal to their proficiency bonus of abilities that recover on a long rest. ([[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Hexcrawl Resting Rules\|details]])
Taking a long rest will most likely provoke rolls on the [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Overclock\|Overclock]] or [[Hexcrawl Rules/Event Clocks/Underclock\|Underclock]] depending in where it is taken. 

If a long rest is taken in a secret or hidden location characters gain the following additional benefits:
- Recover a number of hit dice equal to their character level divided by 4 rounded down, minimum 1.
If a long rest is taken in an allied [[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Glossary/Outpost\|Outpost]] they gain the following improved benefits instead:
- Regain all your missing hit points.
- Recover a number of hit dice equal to half their character level rounded down, minimum 1.

If a long rest is taken in an allied [[Hexcrawl Rules/Sanctuary/Sanctuary\|Sanctuary]] a character gains the following benefits:
- Regains all their missing hit points.
- Regains all their missing hit dice.
- Regains all uses of their abilities that recover on a short or long rest.
- Reduce their level of exhaustion by 1.
##### Poor Quality Long Rest
Long resting without taking a camp watch, represented by going to sleep without sufficient shelter and food, leads to poor recovery. After completing a poor quality long rest a characters may:
- Spend a hit dice to roll the dice and regain hp equal to the result plus the characters Constitution modifier.
- Regain one use of an ability that recovers on a short rest (option, half or full proficiency bonus uses).
- Spend a hit dice to recover proficiency bonus amount of uses of short rest abilities. (Warlock 1 slot equals one hit dice, paladin 1 use of lay of hands equals 5hp).
- Spend ***one*** hit dice to regain a number of uses equal to their proficiency bonus of abilities that recover on a long rest. ([[Hexcrawl Rules/Rules & Glossary/Hexcrawl Resting Rules\|details]])
##### Interrupted Rests
Any form of rest is interrupted by:
- Rolling initiative.
- Casting a spell with a spell slot.
- Taking Damage.
Once interrupted from a rest, a character must start the rest over from the beginning to gain the benefits of completing a rest.
### Animals
A party can bring along mounts, pack animals or animal companions in their travels. Below are the rules for bringing animals and providing additional food and water whilst traveling.
#### Bringing Pack Animals
A party member is designated as the pack-puller and is responsible for managing the parties pack animals. A pack-puller can lead a number of animals equal to their passive Wisdom (Animal Handling) score. (This number includes the pack-puller’s mount, if any.)
#### Grazing Animals
Some animals (like horses) can simply graze for sustenance. In an appropriate biome (as determined by the GM), they will be fully fed as long as they are allowed to graze for one watch per day. In a sparse biome, they must graze for two watches per day and it may be necessary to also provide fresh water for them.
